## Background
With the new buildings out back (more on this in an upcoming post), we decided it was time to organize the pool shed a bit better.  I had rigged up a flimsy shelf with big-box-store brackets, but stuff kept falling off, so it was time for something better.

## The Design
SketchUp was surprisingly helpful here.  It usually is, but in this case, the design helped to flesh out a few things that might be a concern.  More on that below.
{% include figure.html img="pool-mat-shelf-sketchup.avif" align="center" %}

It is basically all plywood held together with Dominos.  It has lots of polyurethane to keep the water out; it is holding pool things, after all.

## Construction
There really is something special about working with nice Baltic birch plywood.  It feels like a quality material because it is.  Pricey, yes, but worth it, in my opinion: strong, stable, straight, and very workable, with almost no voids.  The cheap stuff always disappoints and frustrates, often in the middle of the project.  Grrrr.

{% include figure.html img="IMG_1818.avif" align="left" %}

### The Basics
The back, bottom, and front were pretty straightforward.  I cut them all to the same length, put the Domino holes in the right places, and glued them together.

Not much to see here. 

{% include clear-float.html %}

{% include figure.html img="IMG_1819.avif" align="right" %}

### Advanced Dominos

The hard part in this one comes the moment you try to do multilevel joinery with the Domino.  As you can see from the picture here, there are two vertical rows of Dominos on the sides and one horizontal row at the bottom.

When you do this, it becomes very tricky because your measurements now have to be almost spot on.  The pieces going into these domino holes have already been cut to length and glued up, so you have to cut the domino holes in this piece in exactly the right spot, both horizontally and vertically.

The thing is, you are referencing off three different surfaces, although one always prefers to reference off the same surface if possible.  I couldn't do that here.

Cutting the angled sides was fun. I used a track saw.  Nothing fancy: I just marked a line where the cut was to go, dropped the track on top, and made the slice.

{% include clear-float.html %}

> **To Domino or not to Domino**: Now the woodworking types out there might be saying, _"Why use Dominos when some pocket holes and screws would do just fine?"_
And you might be right.  It would probably do fine, but would it be as good?  
The dominos add a lot of strength in this particular configuration, something that screws or pocket holes cannot match.  And I like to practice these kinds of things anyway, so you do you, and I'll do me.

## Assembly

{% include figure.html img="IMG_1820.avif" align="left" %}

### Dry-fit

To make sure that everything lined up properly, I did a full dry fit.  A dry fit just means putting it all together without gluing anything first.  Lo and behold, it all lined up.

I think the thing that made it work is that I glued up all the "basic" parts first, then took measurements of that subassembly before cutting the sides.  That way the sides were cut to the exact size of the completed front/back/bottom, and they fit perfectly.

{% include clear-float.html %}

### Clamps

{% include figure-pair.html img1="IMG_1830.avif" img2="IMG_1831.avif"%}

Clamping the front/back/bottom was pretty standard stuff.  

Clamping the sides, however, was a bit more complicated.  First, there was the whole multidimensional-rows-of-Dominos-in-the-sides thing going on, which induced mild panic when trying to fit things in.  Even though the dry fit was successful, putting glue on things changes everything.  Things don't quite fit as nicely and easily as they did when dry, and now you are on a ten-minute timer to get everything together and clamped up.

Second, this shelf is almost 2 metres long (6 ft 6 in).  I do not have any clamps that long, so I had to use a nifty extender thing that joins two shorter clamps together to make one long one.  They are a bit of a pain to set up, but super handy to have in this kind of situation.

## In Production

{% include figure.html img="IMG_1832.avif" align="left" %}

### French Cleat

I used a French cleat to mount the shelf to the side wall of the pool shed.

Cleats are a great way to mount things like this, since all you do is mount and level a small cleat to the wall, then throw the shelf up onto the cleat.

### Mats Installed

Room for several pool mats and even some toys.
