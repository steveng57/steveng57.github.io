## Background

This is the second post in the console series.  Part one was all about the design and creation of the console, you can check it out [here]({% link _posts/woodworking/2026-07-11-console.MD %}).  In this episode we dive into the creation of the crate needed to ship the console 2500km from Ottawa to Evan's place in New Westminster.

This was not trivial as the crates had to be strong enough to survive the journey, but also do it in a moving van surrounded by who-knows-what. 

## The Build

So the first step in all this was to build the palets upon which the crate would ship.  I learned a new moving industry term that your shipment needs to be "forkable".  This doesn't show up in a lot of dictionaries.

> Forkable: An item is forkable if it has enough clearance underneath for forklift forks and is strong enough to withstand lifting
{: .prompt-info .sjg-br}

So these were built to that spec.

{% include figure-pair.html img1="IMG_3030.avif" img2="IMG_3034.avif" %}

Bonus: I got to build these in the new garage, my first woodworking project out there.  Nice setup, didn't need to worry about sawdust, just opened the doors and let it fly into the woods.

### The Crate Frame

The crate frame, which will have plywood strapped to it, was made with 2x2's.  Making it out of 2x4's seemed a bit extreme, turns out that these were more than strong enough and it helped keep the weight down.

### Plywood Wrap

Plywood was wrapped around the frame.  Normally I will go for the good plywood at KJP (now called The Wood Vault), but this is throw away stuff.  So the 9mm thick stuff from the big box store fit the bill nicely here.

This is a pic of the crates in "dry-fit" mode, there is nothing inside.

### Foam Padding

So I took the empty crates apart again and lined them everywhere with solid foam.

You do not want to hear the story about how I fit two giant 4ftx9ft 2in thick foam sheets into my car.  Not entirely sure it was legal so cannot speak any further on that issue.

{% include clear-float.html %}

### Tyvek

Before putting the pieces into the crates, I wrapped them in Tyvek, a waterproof but breathable material to add another layer of protection for the trip.

Debbie was instrumental in this part, as I cannot fold or wrap a Christmas present, let alone something this big.

{% include figure.html img="IMG_3064.avif" align = "center" %}

{% include clear-float.html %}

{% include figure.html img="IMG_3070.avif" align="left"%}

### Strapping

Once the pieces had been inserted into their crates, the lids screwed on and set on top of the palets, they needed to be secured.

I found this strapping rig on Amazon that had the straps, buckles, tensioner, and crimper all in the same kit.  It worked really well and was a lot easier than I thought it was going to be.  Took me about 15 minutes for the first strap, and about three minutes for each one after that.
